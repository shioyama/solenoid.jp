**Solenoid** is a cafe and event space located in the western district of [Hakodate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakodate), a city at the southern tip of Hokkaido in northern Japan. Run by Canadian programmer [Chris Salzberg](https://chrissalzberg.com) (i.e. me), Solenoid functions as a personal office most days of the week, but on Friday evenings transforms into a cafe and bar for anyone to visit. (Occasionally I also open it on other days of the week, usually on the weekend.)

The name "Solenoid" comes from a [2015 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid_(novel)){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} by Romanian writer [Mircea Cărtărescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_C%C4%83rt%C4%83rescu){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, one of my favourite books of all time. *Solenoid* tells the story of a Romanian elementary school teacher and failed writer against the backdrop of a surrealistic 1970s Bucharest, in a style reminiscent of Kafka and Borges. Since its translation to English in 2022, it has gained international acclaim as an absurdist masterpiece, winning the 2022 Los Angeles Times Book prize for fiction and the 2024 International Dublin Literary Award, among others.

While Cărtărescu's novel is the original inspiration for the name Solenoid, there is another overlapping meaning: a [solenoid valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid_valve){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} is an essential part of most modern espresso machines, and espresso drinks are an essential item on Solenoid's menu. The original meaning of the term [solenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} in physics is a coil-shaped device that generates a magnetic field when electric current flows through it.

Hakodate is a unique city that I recommend to anyone visiting Japan for the first time. At the right place and time of day, it has a vibe that feels to me like scenes from Cărtărescu's novel. If you happen to be making the trip here, come by and say hello: Solenoid is the only cafe I know of in the tourist area of Hakodate to be run by an international resident, and I like to think it offers a unique window into this intriguing city. Check the [calendar](/en/calendar) for opening days, and the [access](/en/access) page for hours and directions.
